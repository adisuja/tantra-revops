The Book-a-Call Page (With Case Studies)

A page with only a calendar asks for trust it hasn't earned. Put the calendar, the questions, the case studies and what happens on the call on one page, so buyers see the proof before they book.

The page, block by block

1The promise of the call: what 30 minutes gets them, in one line
2The calendar, on the first screen, in their time zone
Above the fold on a laptop
3The booking questions (they qualify the call and double as your prep)
4Case studies by stage and industry
5The proof: numbers from client trackers, testimonials word for word
6What happens on the call

The booking questions

QuestionWhy it is there
Company name and websiteSo you can look before the call
Roughly how much revenue does the business bring in a month? (Under $10K / $10K to $25K / $25K to $100K / $100K to $500K / Over $500K)Matches them to the right case studies
Are you ready to invest in growth in the next 12 months? (Yes / Not yet)A "not yet" never blocks the booking
Where do most of your new customers come from today?Tells you which channel to look at first
What is the one thing stopping revenue growing faster right now?Your first question on the call
Who signs off on an investment like this?Ask them to bring that person

A "not yet" on budget never blocks the booking. On the call, ask: "Is that timing, or budget?"

The proof rules

1

Numbers only from the client's own tracker

Never a modelled or estimated figure. No number is better than a soft one.

2

Testimonials word for word, names only with a written yes

Quote people exactly as they gave it. A client without a written yes is "a B2B training company".

What happens on the call

  • 30 minutes on video: we walk your funnel and stop where the drop is worst. Bring the person who signs off.
  • You leave with a next step, or a straight answer that it isn't a fit.
