The Calendar Invite Blueprint

A calendar invite lands in your buyer's own calendar. Written like a meeting, it gets read like one. Here is the template, the 4 rules and the prompt that writes it for your event.

The template

Title · under 60 characters
[Topic]: [the outcome they leave with]
Description
For [buyer role] at [kind of company]. {event_date}, {event_time} [time zone]. You leave with [the one thing they walk away with]. [Detail 1: what we cover first] [Detail 2: what we build or show] [Detail 3: how long it takes and who runs it] Join here: {join_link} Not for you? Decline this invite and you won't get another one from me. {sender_name}

The merge fields

{event_date} {event_time} {join_link} {sender_name}

Spell them exactly as your tool expects. A misspelled field goes out as raw text.

The preview is the invite

Most people only see the title and the first two lines. Those three lines must say who it is for and what they leave with.

The 4 rules every invite follows

1

Send it 8 to 12 days before the event

12 days out is the earliest, 8 days the latest. Send from your warmed outreach mailboxes, never your main domain.

2

Keep the title under 60 characters

Name the topic and the outcome the way a meeting would be named. No $, no %, no "free". Short enough to read in a calendar grid.

3

Make the first two lines do the work

Line 1: who it is for, with the time zone. Line 2: what they leave with. Details and the link come after.

4

Add one line that makes declining easy

Saying no should take one click. It keeps complaints down, and a No stays a No.

Before you send

  • A test invite to my own calendar shows the promise in the preview.
  • The time is right in my own time zone, and matches my event platform.
  • The link works and is tagged utm_source=calendar.
  • The description is short enough that the promise stays in the preview.

Two versions, one offer

Version A: benefit-led

The title and line 2 lead with the outcome your buyer wants.

Version B: problem-led

The title and line 2 lead with the objection, in your buyer's own words.

Same event, same link. Only the angle changes, so you learn which one your buyers respond to.

The AI prompt that writes the invite

Paste it into any AI assistant. Fill the three [FILL] gaps with your own event, link and objection.

Write my cold calendar invite
Write a cold calendar invite for [FILL: my event line].
Title: the topic and the outcome, like a meeting name, under 60 characters, no $, % or "free".
Description: line 1 = who it is for and the time zone; line 2 = what they leave with. Then 3 short lines of detail, the link [FILL: registration link with utm_source=calendar], and one line that makes declining easy.
Two versions: A benefit-led, B problem-led using "[FILL: objection]".
Use my Funnel Brief. No invented numbers, names or results.

How to use it

1

Give it your facts first

Your offer, your buyer, your event line and your proof. The prompt calls this your Funnel Brief. One page is enough.

2

Run the prompt, then check it against page 2

Count the title. Read only the first two lines. Would you know who it is for and what you leave with?

3

Send yourself a test before anyone else

Open it on your phone calendar. If the promise is cut off, shorten the description and test again.

If a fact is missing

Tell the AI to write [FILL] in its place. A made-up number in an invite costs you more than a blank ever will.
