The AI SDR System

An AI SDR set up in Claude. It finds the people who fit, checks each company, sorts them into segments, drafts the first message and lines up the follow-ups. Your people approve the messages and take the calls.

Who does what

StepAI doesA person does
1 FindMerges every source into one list: your CRM, LinkedIn searches, past registrantsSets the fit rules and the exclusions
2 CheckReads each company's site and recent posts, cuts the wrong fitsChecks the first 25 rows before trusting the rest
3 SortPuts each person in the segment their message is written forApproves the segments
4 WriteDrafts one message per segment, merge fields only, A and B versionsApproves every message before it goes out
5 Follow upQueues the follow-ups on each channel's timingAnswers every real reply the same day, in their own words
6 CallNothing. This part is yoursTakes the calls

Set it up in Claude

1

One project, one brief

Make one Claude project. Put your offer, your buyer, your fit rules, your exclusions and your proof in one brief file. Every prompt reads it first.

2

One list

Merge your sources before anything else and remove duplicates by email.

3

Separate sending domains

Send from look-alike domains, never your main one, after a two-week warm-up, 8am to 8pm their time.

4

Nothing goes out without a yes

The AI drafts and queues. Sending, buying or deleting waits for your own words.

The timing it follows

ChannelThe rule
Cold email5 steps on days 0, 3, 6, 9 and 13; only step 1 has a subject; steps 2 to 5 under 80 words; one link
LinkedInConnection note under 300 characters; 15 to 20 requests a day per profile; withdraw after 14 days; one tool per profile
Calendar inviteSent 8 to 12 days out; title under 60 characters; one line that makes declining easy

The AI SDR System

Prompt 1: sort the list

Sort my list into segments
YOUR TASK
Sort every buyer in the list below into one of my segments, so each segment can get its own invite.

THE FACTS TO USE
My segments: [FILL: segment name, who is in it (role and kind of company), and what just changed for them, one line each]
My fit rules: [FILL: who must be in the list, who must never be]

RULES
- Read each company's website and the person's recent posts before you sort them.
- One segment per person. If nobody fits, write NONE and the reason. Never guess.

WHAT TO RETURN
A table: first name, company, role, segment, one-line reason.

CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
Every row has exactly one segment or NONE. No row has a made-up fact.

THE LIST
[PASTE: first_name, company, website, role, linkedin_url]

Prompt 2: the first message

Write the first message per segment
YOUR TASK
Write the first message to each segment of my list, for [FILL: my event line or offer].

THE FACTS TO USE
My segments and the problem each one has: [FILL]
What they get: [FILL]

RULES
- Merge fields only: {first_name}, {company}, {role}. No line written for one person.
- Two versions per segment: A leads with the outcome, B with the objection in the buyer's own words.
- One link. Under 80 words. End with an easy way to say no.
- A missing fact becomes [FILL]. Never invent a number or a client.

WHAT TO RETURN
For each segment: version A and version B, with a subject line.

CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
Each message reads well even if {company} is empty.

Before it sends

  • The first 25 sorted rows look right.
  • Every message was approved by a person.
  • A reply stops the sequence for that person.
  • Anyone who says no comes off the list for good.
