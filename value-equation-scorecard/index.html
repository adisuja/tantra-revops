The Value Equation Scorecard

How much your offer is worth to a buyer comes down to four levers. Score each one out of 10 the way a sceptic would, fix the two lowest, then rewrite the offer. The value equation is Alex Hormozi's, from $100M Offers.

The equation
Value = (Dream outcome × Likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort)

Score your offer

10 is best for the buyer on every lever: a big, specific outcome, a believable promise, a short wait, little effort.

LeverThe sceptic's questionScore /10One change that raises it
Dream outcomeWhat exactly do I get, as a number with a unit and a period?
LikelihoodWhy would it work for me? Show me proof I can check in ten seconds.
Time delayHow long until I see the first result?
EffortHow much of this do I have to do myself?
TotalOut of 40

Fix the two lowest, not the highest

Sceptics punish big promises. Cutting time and effort is easier to believe, and harder for anyone to copy, than a bigger outcome.

The Value Equation Scorecard

Changes that move each lever

1

Dream outcome

Say it as a number with a unit and a period: "10 booked calls a month", not "more pipeline".

2

Likelihood

Put one piece of proof a sceptic can check in ten seconds next to the promise: a name, a number, a date.

3

Time delay

Give them a first win inside week one, even if the full result takes months.

4

Effort

Do one step for them. Every step you take off their plate means less effort for them, and a higher score.

The prompt that scores and rewrites it

Score and rewrite my offer
YOUR TASK
Score my offer on the value equation the way a sceptical buyer would, then rewrite it.

THE FACTS TO USE
My offer: [FILL: who it is for, what they get, how long it takes, what they have to do]
My proof: [FILL: real results with a number, a name and a date, or leave empty]

RULES
- Score each lever out of 10, where 10 is best for the buyer: dream outcome (big and specific), likelihood (believable), time delay (short), effort (low).
- Be as hard as a sceptic. Explain each score in one line.
- For the two lowest levers, write one concrete change each: a number and a deadline, proof a sceptic can check in ten seconds, a win inside week one, or one step done for the buyer.
- Never invent proof. A missing fact becomes [FILL].

WHAT TO RETURN
The four scores and the total out of 40, the two changes, then the offer rewritten as one sentence.

CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
The rewrite cuts time or effort before it makes a bigger promise.

Before you use the new offer

  • The outcome has a number, a unit and a period.
  • The proof is real and has a source. No proof yet means none on the page.
  • I read the new sentence to someone outside the business and they had no follow-up question.
